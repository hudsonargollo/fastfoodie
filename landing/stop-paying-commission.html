Five things an app order costs you that your own order does not

The apps do something useful: they put your kitchen in front of people who had never heard of you. What follows is what happens on every order after that one.

The percentage, every single time

The commission is charged per order, not per new customer. The hundredth order from your best customer costs you exactly what the first one did.

Cost: the same slice, sold to you again and again

The customer never becomes yours

The phone number, the address and the history sit in the platform’s account. You cannot message that person without buying the same sale again.

Cost: repeat sales you have to re-purchase

Your own volume is invisible

You can see the orders that came through the app. You cannot see the customer who walks in, or the one who rings the shop, or which of the three is worth the most.

Cost: a menu and a budget built on part of the picture

Every rule change lands on you

Commission, ranking, delivery pricing and the terms of the listing are decided by someone else and change on someone else’s schedule.

Cost: your margin moves without you deciding anything

Theirs is the shop window

Your menu appears inside their layout, next to your competitors, at their prices. The customer is loyal to the app that remembers their address — not to your kitchen.

Cost: a relationship that is rented, not owned

Where every number on this page comes from

You should check this arithmetic before you believe any of it. These are the formulas, and which inputs are yours rather than mine.

commission today    = app orders × ticket × commission%
orders moved         = app orders × share migrated
you stop paying   = orders moved × ticket × commission%
software fee        = orders moved × Bs 1
your delivery cost = orders moved × Bs per order you enter
net you keep         = you stop paying − software fee − your delivery cost
Plan 2 payback     = ($1,000 × rate) ÷ net    Plan 1 payback = ($2,000 × rate) ÷ net
retention estimate = orders moved × extra orders a year × ticket × commission%

  • Yours to correct: app orders, ticket, commission, share migrated, your own delivery cost, repeat orders, exchange rate.
  • Mine, and defaults: 400 app orders, Bs 60 ticket, 25% commission, 35% migrated, Bs 0 own delivery, 2 extra orders a year, Bs 11.50 per USD. The 11.50 convention is shared with the rest of the pricing so both documents agree.
  • Fixed by the proposal: Bs 1 per order, $150 per month, $1,000 Plan 2 upfront, $2,000 Plan 1.
  • What the Bs 1 is charged on: every order the system processes. This calculator charges it on the migrated orders only — if your app orders also run through the intake automation, as most clients’ do, add Bs 1 for each of those too. On the fixed $150 / month option none of this moves, because volume changes nothing.
  • Deliberately not here: any named platform’s published rate, ranking rules or terms. We do not claim to know them, and nothing on this page depends on them.
  • Where the net goes negative: if delivering an order yourself costs more than the commission you save, the calculator says so instead of hiding it. That is a real answer, not a failure.

How it actually happens

A ramp, not a switch

Kitchens that do this badly lose volume and panic. Kitchens that do it well never ask a customer to change habits they like — they just make the second order cheaper for both sides.

1

Keep every app listing live

Nothing leaves. Your own channel is opened, your staff are trained on one working session, and orders from both worlds arrive in the same system. Go-live is usually within a week of signing.

2

Move the repeat orders first

The customer who has ordered twice already does not need the app’s discovery. A card in the bag, a WhatsApp number, one good reason to order direct next time.

3

Measure it, then decide

You see what actually moved, what it saved, and what the app still brings you. Most kitchens land on keeping the apps for new customers and pushing repeat volume to their own channel.

What does not move, and should not be pretended

  • Discovery. A platform that puts you in front of strangers is doing something you cannot do by messaging your own list. Keep paying for that where it works.
  • Delivery logistics, if you used their riders. That cost crosses to your side of the ledger. Put it in the advanced slider before you argue with anyone.
  • Customer inertia. Some people will always tap the app that remembers their address. The 35% default on this page is a ceiling to argue with, not a guarantee.

Fair questions

What owners ask before they believe this

Won’t customers just keep ordering through the app?

For the first order, usually yes — that is what the app is for. This is about the second order and onwards, where the customer already knows your food and the app is only remembering an address. Give them one reason, keep the price the same, and measure what actually moves instead of assuming it will not.

Does this mean cancelling PedidosYa, Uber Eats or Rappi?

No, and a page that told you to would be selling you something. Keep the listings that bring strangers in. The argument on this page is only about orders from people who already came back to you once.

You do not know my commission rate, so why should I trust the numbers?

Correct — we do not, and this page never claims a platform’s rate. That is why commission is a slider you set from your own statement, and why every figure recalculates around it. If your real rate is lower, the saving shrinks in front of you.

What if I used the app’s riders?

Then delivering those orders costs you something it did not cost before, and any page that ignored that was lying to you. Put a per-order figure in the advanced slider — rider pay, fuel, or what you pay a courier per drop. If the net goes negative, this page says so plainly rather than hiding it.

Do I pay the Bs 1 fee on orders that stay on the app?

If the order passes through the system, it counts — that is how the per-order option is defined. Most kitchens route everything through it, because capturing the app orders is half the reason for buying it. At 400 orders a month that is Bs 400, roughly $35 — against Bs 6,000 of commission on the same volume. If you would rather your fee not move with your volume at all, the alternative on Plan 2 is the fixed $150 / month.

What do I actually get: a list, or customers who order again?

The software gives you the record — that is the asset. Turning it into orders is work: offers, timing, segments. That is the marketing add-on, and you can do it yourself if you would rather. We will tell you which of the two you are signing up for before you sign.

How long before any of this shows in my numbers?

Usually within a week of signing to go live, because the work is configuration, not development. The migration itself is slower than the setup: weeks, not days, and it is measured monthly rather than hoped for.

What does this cost me?

Two ways to buy: one payment covering a full year of licence with no monthly fee, or a lower upfront amount with either a fixed monthly fee or Bs 1 per order. At Bs 1, a Bs 60 order paying 25% commission covers its own software fee about fifteen times over.
